# Frequently Asked Practical Questions (FAQ)

**Source:** Compiled from SHSU Cost of Attendance and SHSU Transfer Process Guide
**URL:** https://www.shsu.edu/cost-aid/cost-attendance ; https://umawi1427.github.io/shsu-transfer-process/step6.html

**Q: How much money do I need before arriving?**
For the I-20/F-1 visa process, a bank statement showing at least $39,110 USD available is required. The official SHSU Cost of Attendance for a full-time nonresident undergraduate (2026-2027) is $41,860/year, covering tuition and fees ($23,810), food and housing ($12,804), books ($1,380), transportation ($2,004), and personal expenses ($1,792). Before arrival, students also pay application/visa fees: ApplyTexas $90, SEVIS fee $350, MRV visa fee ~$185 (about $625 total).

**Q: How much do I spend per month living in Huntsville?**
General cost-of-living estimate for one person in Huntsville, TX: about $1,900/month total with rent, or about $826/month without rent (groceries, utilities, transportation, dining out). On-campus meal plans range from $635 to $2,750 per semester depending on the plan tier.

**Q: What if I can't find a job?**
International students can search on-campus jobs via PeopleAdmin (shsu.peopleadmin.com) and Aramark.com. Work is limited to 20 hours/week during Fall/Spring and up to 28 hours/week in Summer (if enrollment requirements are met). The SHSU Career Success Center (careersuccesscenter@shsu.edu) also helps with resumes, mock interviews, and job search strategies. If facing financial hardship, the Bearkat Emergency Fund provides temporary assistance.

**Q: Which dorm/residence hall should I choose, and is off-campus or on-campus cheaper?**
On-campus residence halls include Belvin-Buchanan, Elliott, Estill, Jackson-Shaver, Lone Star, Piney Woods, Raven Village, Sam Houston Village, San Jacinto, and White Hall, plus apartment communities (Bearkat Village, Campus Edge, Copper Village). Students entering within 1 year of high school graduation must live on campus their first year. Off-campus options in Huntsville (see the SHSU off-campus housing list) generally start lower — e.g., The Grove $439-$569/month — than combined on-campus room+board costs, though on-campus includes proximity, included utilities/dining, and no separate lease.

**Q: Is a meal plan mandatory?**
Yes for first-year students living in most residence halls — they default to the All Access plan. Meal plans are not required for residents of Bearkat Village, Campus Edge, Copper Village Apartments, or San Jacinto Studio Apartments, or for off-campus/commuter students (who may optionally choose a commuter plan such as Bearkat Value, Lite, or Flex).

**URL:** https://www.shsu.edu/cost-aid/types-of-aid/scholarships

**Q: What if I don't get a scholarship?**
Transfer students with 30-89 transferable hours and a 2.75+ GPA automatically receive the Bearkat Transfer Scholarship ($2,000/year) upon Fall admission — no separate application needed. Other scholarships (National Merit, Smith-Hutson, Honors, SHSU Local) require specific eligibility. Without at least a $1,000 competitive scholarship, students do not qualify for the Non-Resident Tuition Waiver and continue paying the nonresident/international tuition rate ($649/credit hour vs. $244/credit hour for residents).

**Q: Does my Turkish bank card work in the U.S.?**
Bank of America is recommended due to a special agreement with Turkish TEB Bank, allowing withdrawals of up to $300/day from Bank of America ATMs without commission fees. Students typically also open a U.S. Bank of America account after arrival (requires passport, F-1 visa, I-20, proof of U.S. address, and SSN or proof of application).

**Q: What should I watch out for in my first week/semester?**
Before registering, complete an academic advisor appointment, the TSI Exam, TB Questionnaire, and Meningitis Vaccine proof. After registering, complete the Campus Prevention Training (Title IX, ~2 hours) within about a week to avoid a registration hold — contact tixhold@shsu.edu with issues. Decide about the Bearkat Bundle textbook program at least 30 days before classes start if opting out.

**Q: Can I work after graduation?**
F-1 students may apply for Optional Practical Training (OPT), allowing work in their field of study for up to 12 months after graduation (requires DSO recommendation and USCIS approval). Curricular Practical Training (CPT) is available during studies, typically after two full-time semesters, and must be tied to a course for academic credit.

**Q: What happens during summer/breaks, and is orientation mandatory?**
International student orientation is mandatory and has two parts: virtual Blackboard modules (12 modules, 80%+ quiz score each) and an in-person session at the Farrington Building (bring passport, visa, I-20, I-94). Missing check-in with required documents can result in SEVIS record termination and loss of F-1 status.
